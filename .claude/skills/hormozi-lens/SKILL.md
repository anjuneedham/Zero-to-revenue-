# Hormozi lens

A thinking tool, not a gospel. These frameworks come from one practitioner's books ($100M Offers, $100M Leads, $100M Money Models) and are paraphrased here. They are heuristics that tend to work for service and software businesses selling to people with a painful problem and money to fix it. **Any benchmark quoted below is a rule of thumb to test, not a fact about Jamaica or about Jay's markets.**

Mentor rule: after every use, tell Jay *why* the weakest lever matters in plain language, then give the one next action.

## Step 0: refuse to guess

Fill in what is known and write **UNKNOWN** for the rest. Never invent prices, conversion rates, churn or customer counts. Unknowns become "go find out" actions (usually a customer conversation). Missing data is itself the finding.

## 1. Pick a starving crowd first

A great offer to people who don't hurt, can't pay, can't be reached or are shrinking is still a bad business. Score the market 1-5 on each:

1. **Pain**: how much does this problem cost them (money, time, embarrassment) *today*?
2. **Purchasing power**: can they actually pay, in JMD or USD, and how do they pay (cash, bank transfer, WhatsApp)?
3. **Easy to target**: can Jay name where to find 100 of them this week (a market, a WhatsApp group, a trade association)?
4. **Growing**: is the group getting bigger?

Low purchasing power is common for smallholder farmers. If so, the farmer side stays free and the money comes from the other side (buyers, suppliers, premium add-ons).

## 2. Value Equation (score every offer)

**Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice)**

Score each lever 1-10 (10 = great for the customer; for the two denominator items, 10 = fast / effortless):

| Lever | Question | How to move it |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | Is the result specific, big, and in *their* words? | Name the result (jobs won, kg sold, first 10 sales), not the feature |
| Likelihood | Why should they believe it will work *for them*? | Proof: local testimonials, photos, guarantee, demo on their own data |
| Time delay | How long until they see the first win? | Ship a first win in days: done-for-you setup, templates, quick-start |
| Effort & sacrifice | What do they have to do, learn, pay upfront, or risk? | Do it for them, WhatsApp-first, free trial, pay-after-result |

Output the four scores, the **weakest lever**, and 2-3 concrete changes. Improve the weakest lever first; polishing the strongest one is wasted effort.

## 3. Grand Slam Offer (build or rebuild)

1. Write the **dream outcome** for one specific avatar.
2. List every **obstacle** between them and that outcome (before, during, after buying).
3. Turn each obstacle into a **solution**, then into a deliverable (checklist, template, call, done-for-you task).
4. **Trim** low-value / high-cost items; **stack** what remains with a clear name and price anchor.
5. Add **risk reversal** that costs little but removes fear: unconditional (money back), conditional (do X, get result or refund), or pay-on-results. Only promise what Jay can actually deliver; check what real fulfilment costs first.
6. Add honest **scarcity/urgency** (real capacity limits, founding-member cohort). Never fake it.
7. **Name it** so the avatar recognises themselves: who it's for, the result, a timeframe, and what's included.

Use the `offers`, `pricing` and `copywriting` skills to flesh out each piece.

## 4. Core Four (how people find the offer)

Rank channels by cost, in this order, because Jay has little cash:

1. **Warm outreach**: people who already know Jay. Message them one-to-one, daily.
2. **Post free content**: useful posts/videos/WhatsApp statuses that demonstrate the result.
3. **Cold outreach**: personalised messages and calls to strangers in the target group (see `cold-email`, `prospecting`).
4. **Paid ads**: only after an offer converts organically (see `ads`, `ad-creative`).

Plus three "lead getters" for later: **employees/helpers**, **agencies**, and **affiliates/partners** (this is where BuildFlow's health-professional revenue share fits; see `partnerships-architect`).

Output a weekly plan with a **volume target per channel** (e.g. 20 warm messages, 20 cold contacts, 3 posts per day). Volume beats cleverness at the start; quality improves with reps.

## 5. CLOSER (sales conversation)

Use for calls, WhatsApp voice notes, demos and in-person visits:

- **C**larify why they're here ("What made you reply / book this?")
- **L**abel the problem in their words and get a "yes, exactly"
- **O**verview what they have tried before and what it cost them
- **S**ell the vacation: describe life after the problem is solved (outcome, not features)
- **E**xplain away concerns (price, time, trust, "I need to ask someone"); treat each as an unresolved obstacle to the outcome
- **R**einforce the decision: confirm next steps, payment method, start date

Ask before pitching; listen for the customer's own words and reuse them. Pair with `sales-enablement` for objection handling and `interview-script` when the goal is learning, not selling.

## 6. Money maths: LTV, CAC, payback

- **LTV** = average gross margin per period x average lifetime (or margin / churn rate).
- **CAC** = all acquisition cost (including Jay's time, priced at an honest hourly value) / new customers.
- Rules of thumb to *test*: LTV:CAC of about 3:1 or better, and acquisition cost paid back within roughly 30-90 days when cash is tight. With little cash, short payback matters more than a high LTV on paper.
- Raise LTV via retention, price tests, expansion (extra users, upgrades) and annual prepay. Use `saas-metrics-coach`, `startup-metrics-framework` and `churn-prevention` for the numbers.

## 7. Constraint diagnosis ("what do I fix first?")

Walk the chain in order and stop at the first weak link; fix only that:

**Market -> Offer -> Leads -> Conversion -> Delivery -> Retention/LTV -> Cash**

- Few people see it -> a leads problem.
- People see it but don't buy -> an offer or conversion problem (run section 2).
- People buy but don't succeed or stay -> delivery/onboarding/retention problem.
- Customers stay but cash is tight -> pricing, payment terms, or payback problem.

State the constraint in one sentence, the evidence for it, and the single experiment that would prove it wrong.

## Output format

1. Market score (4 numbers) and verdict.
2. Value Equation table with scores, weakest lever, 2-3 fixes.
3. Offer rewrite or stack (if asked).
4. Channel plan with weekly volumes.
5. Constraint sentence + one experiment + how to measure it.
6. **Needs verification**: every number or claim that is not from Jay's own data.

## Per-venture reminders

- **Agriloop**: farmers are the supply, not the payer. Sell *buyers* a way to find reliable supply; sell farmers *access to buyers*. See `agriloop-growth`.
- **BuildFlow**: contractors are busy and distrust software. Lead with "your leads are yours, flat price, WhatsApp-first" and a done-for-you start. See `buildflow-growth`.
- **Fashion brand**: the offer is mostly brand and proof; test demand before holding stock. See `fashion-launch`.
- **YardScale agency**: sell outcomes with fixed scope and a first win in days. Proposals: `contract-and-proposal-writer`.
- **Trading / investing**: this lens does **not** apply to the strategy itself. Never use it to "sell" a bot or to justify risk. See `trading-lab` and `investing-plan`.
