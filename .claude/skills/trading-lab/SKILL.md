# Trading lab

Goal (priority 3): a bot that is **profitable, proven on paper before real money.** This skill is a research and risk-control process. It is **not** a promise that a profitable bot exists.

## Be honest first (tell Jay this plainly)

- Most retail trading bots lose money, and many that look profitable in a backtest are overfitted. No result is guaranteed, and past results do not predict future ones.
- The default assumption (the null hypothesis) is **"this strategy has no edge."** The job of every test is to try to *disprove the edge*, not to confirm it. If the evidence does not clearly beat the null, the answer is NO-GO.
- **Deriv synthetic indices** (Volatility, Step, etc.) are generated by software random-number processes (verify the current wording in Deriv's own documentation). If prices are close to a memoryless random walk, patterns such as breakouts and retests have **no structural reason to pay**, while spreads/commissions are a certain cost. That makes a positive-expectancy strategy on synthetics a high bar, and the burden of proof is on the strategy. Today `mq5` has **no backtest results, paper-trading journal or statistics** (per `docs/master/HANDOFF.md`), so there is currently no evidence of an edge.
- Leverage multiplies both wins and losses. Money put in must be money Jay can afford to lose completely.

## Hard safety rules (non-negotiable; Claude must follow them)

1. **Claude never places live orders, never holds, requests, stores or pastes exchange/broker keys, API tokens or passwords.** Do not ask Jay to paste a Deriv token into chat or commit it. Secrets live only in the trading platform or an untracked local env file.
2. Code defaults to **demo/paper/backtest**. Any live switch must be an explicit human toggle with a visible warning, never the default, and never set by Claude.
3. Only tighten risk limits, never loosen them, after seeing results. Changing a limit to make a result pass is a failure of the process.
4. Run the vendored scripts only on local data. Any external data download needs Jay's approval and must not use real-account credentials.

## The pipeline (each stage has a gate; log everything)

1. **Hypothesis**: one sentence, written *before* testing: what market behaviour, why it should persist, and what would prove it wrong (`strategy-framework`). Note that on synthetics "why" may have no answer.
2. **Data**: source, period, timeframe, how gaps are handled. Keep a **final holdout** (at least the most recent 30%) sealed until the last step.
3. **Backtest with realistic costs** (`backtesting-frameworks`, `backtest-expert`, `mt5-robot-tester` for MT5 Strategy Tester runs): spread, commission, swap, slippage, minimum lot, stop-level rules, no look-ahead, every trade counted. Count **every variant you try** (multiple-testing penalty: the more you try, the likelier a lucky result).
4. **Robustness**: change each parameter +/-20-30%. If performance collapses, it was curve-fit. Test other symbols/periods and a stress period.
5. **Walk-forward and out-of-sample** (`walk-forward-validation`): optimise on a window, test on the next unseen window, roll forward. Report out-of-sample (OOS) results separately, never blended.
6. **Monte Carlo / permutation test**: shuffle or resample trades, and compare with random entries on the same data. Is the strategy distinguishable from luck? (`backtest-expert`, `risk-metrics-calculation`)
7. **Position sizing and risk** (`position-sizer`, `kelly-criterion`: use at most a quarter to half Kelly, and never Kelly on an unproven edge).
8. **Paper trading** on a demo account with the live bot code and a journal (`trade-journal`). See minimums below.
9. **Go/no-go review** using [go-no-go-checklist.md](go-no-go-checklist.md). Any unchecked box is NO-GO.
10. **Micro-live** (only after GO): the smallest size the platform allows, hard kill-switch armed, extra waiting period, then review again.

## Performance statistics to report (formulas and traps)

- **Expectancy** per trade = win% x average win - loss% x average loss, in R-multiples (R = initial risk). Must be positive **after costs** with a confidence interval (bootstrap) whose lower bound is above zero.
- **Profit factor** = gross profit / gross loss. A very high number from a few trades is a warning sign, not a success.
- **Sharpe / Sortino**: state period, risk-free assumption and annualisation; they are unstable on small samples.
- **Max drawdown** (peak-to-trough, equity based) and **drawdown duration**; also longest losing streak. Plan for a future drawdown larger than any in the backtest.
- **Trade count**: below roughly 100 independent OOS trades, conclusions are weak; say so.
- Report with and without costs so Jay can see how much the edge depends on assumptions.

## Hard limits (defaults; can only be tightened)

| Rule | Default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Risk per trade | <= 1% of account equity (0.25-0.5% in paper and micro-live) | Survive losing streaks |
| Daily loss limit | <= 3% of equity, bot stops for the day | Stops spirals |
| **Hard max drawdown (kill-switch)** | **10% from equity peak: bot disabled, human review required before restart; absolute ceiling 20%** | Capital preservation; a bot that breaches it is declared failed, not "unlucky" |
| Backtest/OOS max drawdown acceptance | Worst OOS drawdown must be <= half the kill-switch level | Otherwise the kill-switch would trip almost immediately |
| Open risk | <= 3% total at once | Correlated trades fail together |

## Minimum paper-trading period (before any real money)

At least **8 weeks AND at least 100 completed trades** on a demo account (whichever is longer), with the final bot version and **no parameter changes** during the period, a complete journal, and live-like costs. Results must stay within the OOS expectation (e.g. inside the Monte Carlo band). After GO, run **micro-live for at least 4 more weeks** at minimum size before scaling anything.

## Output format

1. Verdict now (almost always "NOT PROVEN yet") with the specific missing evidence.
2. Stage reached, next gate, and the one next action.
3. Statistics table (with costs, OOS separate, confidence intervals, trade count).
4. Risks and what would invalidate the result.
5. **Needs verification**: every claim about Deriv, costs, data quality and numbers not computed from Jay's own logs.
